SAWA

Semantic Affordances for the Web of Agents: bridging Multi-Agent Systems, Semantic Web Services, and Agentic AI

A half-day, discussion-led workshop at ISWC 2026 in Bari, Italy, held on 25 or 26 October 2026 (exact day and room to be confirmed).

Submission guidelines See the programme

Overview

Twenty years ago, the Semantic Web Services community asked how autonomous agents with different ontological commitments could discover, combine, and invoke each other's capabilities. OWL-S, WSMO, and SAWSDL were the answer: formally grounded capability descriptions, explicit mediation across heterogeneous services, and protocols for discovery and composition.

LLM-based agentic AI is now running into the same problem. Frameworks such as the Model Context Protocol and Agent2Agent largely describe tools and services in natural language, and select between them by linguistic similarity rather than formal semantics. That reintroduces failure modes the Semantic Web Services community already spent a decade characterising, and in many cases solving.

SAWA is a half-day, discussion-led workshop that puts the Multi-Agent Systems, Semantic Web Services, and Agentic AI communities in the same room to ask what that older formal work still has to offer, with a particular focus on affordances: what an agent can actually do, given its state and its environment. The aim is a shared research agenda, and a first step toward a follow-up Dagstuhl proposal.

Motivation

The original Semantic Web placed autonomous agents at the centre of a globally distributed, machine-readable knowledge ecosystem. RDF, OWL, SPARQL, and mature reasoning engines followed, alongside a substantial programme of work on service description, mediation, and discovery protocols, all designed for the same open, multi-agent settings that agentic AI systems are now being deployed into at scale.

Recent work has started to draw these threads back together. Polleres and colleagues have traced the historical roots of agentic AI directly back to Semantic Web Services research, and there is growing interest across the Semantic Web and Multi-Agent Systems communities in framing agent, service, and resource discovery through the lens of affordances: formalising what an agent can do, given its state, its goals, and its environment.

SAWA brings the Multi-Agent Systems, Semantic Web Services, and Semantic Web communities together to critically assess what this research legacy offers the design of current LLM-based agentic systems, and where affordance frameworks in particular can provide a grounded alternative to purely linguistic tool and service descriptions.

Topics of interest

  • Revisiting Semantic Web Services, including OWL-S and WSMO, for agentic AI
  • Capability description and ontological grounding for agentic tools and services
  • Agent communication protocols and semantic interoperability
  • Bridging LLM-based and symbolic agent architectures
  • Ontology-based and knowledge graph approaches to planning, symbol grounding, and action execution
  • Human-agent interaction and goal decomposition in agentic environments
  • Epistemic trust, entailment regimes, and closure assumptions in discovering and querying agentic knowledge graphs
  • Agentic AI in domains such as ontology engineering, smart environments, and digital assistants
  • Evaluation benchmarks and testbeds for agentic knowledge systems

Submission and participation

We invite short position statements, up to two pages, on any of the topics above. These will be circulated to registered participants ahead of the workshop, so discussion on the day can start from a shared footing rather than a round of introductions.

You're also welcome to submit a short summary of published or unpublished work relevant to the workshop's themes. We're not asking for original unpublished research: SAWA is a discussion-led workshop, not a paper track, and submissions will be lightly reviewed for relevance and interest rather than technical novelty.

We're aiming for a group of 20 to 30 participants: a mix of senior researchers, early-career researchers, and practitioners building agentic systems in industry, with diversity of career stage, institution, and country a priority. Participation is open to all ISWC 2026 attendees; presenting authors should register for both the workshop and the conference.

Organizers

Terry R. Payne

University of Liverpool, UK

Over 30 years of research in agent-based computing, knowledge-based systems, and ontology-driven service discovery. A founding member of the OWL-S coalition, he helped develop the formal capability description framework for Semantic Web Services. His recent work with Valentina Tamma and Enrico Daga proposes the Agentic Affordance Profile, revisiting OWL-S and WSMO insights for knowledge graph settings.

Expected outcomes

Shared research agenda. A prioritised list of open agentic AI challenges where Semantic Web Services, knowledge graph, and Multi-Agent Systems research can contribute, alongside the opportunities semantically grounded agentic AI offers in return.

Community building. A starting point for a research network spanning semantic, autonomous, and interoperable agentic systems, drawing on the W3C WebAgents Community Group, the Semantic Web Science Association, and researchers building agentic AI infrastructure.

Dagstuhl proposal. A draft proposal for a follow-up Dagstuhl Seminar, where the discussions from the workshop can be developed further into a structured research agenda.

Workshop chairs: Terry R. Payne, Andrei Ciortea, Axel Polleres, and Ilaria Tiddi.
